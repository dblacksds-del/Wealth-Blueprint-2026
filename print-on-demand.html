How to Start Print on Demand for Beginners (Step-by-Step)

Print on demand is a business model where you create original graphic designs and display them on blank items like t-shirts, hoodies, and mugs. When a customer places an order on your online store, a third-party print company prints the item and ships it directly to the customer.

Step-by-Step Execution Blueprint (10 Practical Steps)

Follow this 10-step process to set up your catalog safely without risking intellectual property strikes.

1

Pick a Specific Hobby, Pet, or Profession Niche

Generic t-shirts (like simple smiley faces or random slogans) get lost in massive marketplaces. Choose a specific community that has strong pride: bass guitar players, dachshund dog lovers, mechanical keyboard hobbyists, or plant nursery gardeners.

Tool: A blank Google Doc.
Action: Pick one subculture and list 5 insider jokes, relatable phrases, or terms only members of that group understand.
Finished Result: A focused theme tailored to a dedicated group of buyers.
2

Check Official Trademark Databases Before Designing

Before creating any text design, search official government trademark registers. In the United States, use the USPTO Trademark Electronic Search System (TESS). In the UK, search the UK Intellectual Property Office (UK IPO). In Canada, search CIPO. Never use movie quotes, band lyrics, or sports slogans.

Tool: USPTO TESS (tmsearch.uspto.gov) or UK IPO.
Action: Search your phrase under International Class 025 (Clothing). If it is registered, discard it immediately.
Finished Result: A 100% trademark-safe list of original phrases that protect your store from bans.
3

Create a 4500x5400px Transparent PNG Design

Garment printers require high resolution to avoid blurry or pixelated prints. Open Canva or free browser editor Photopea. Create a custom canvas sized at 4500 x 5400 pixels at 300 DPI. Design your typography or graphic layout and export as a transparent PNG file.

Tool: Canva Free or Photopea (photopea.com).
Action: Design your graphic and export with a transparent background.
Finished Result: A clean, sharp 300 DPI master print file.
4

Create a Free Printify or Printful Account

Sign up for a free merchant account on Printify (printify.com) or Printful (printful.com). Both platforms connect you with automated print facilities across the US, UK, Canada, and Europe with zero monthly subscription fees.

Tool: Printify or Printful registration portal.
Action: Complete account verification and set your primary business currency to USD ($).
Finished Result: An active supplier dashboard ready to sync products to your storefront.
5

Select a Reliable Blank Garment

Never pick the cheapest possible rough cardboard-like shirt. Choose standard, customer-favored blanks such as the Bella+Canvas 3001 (soft ringspun cotton) or Gildan 18000 (heavy blend sweatshirt). Upload your transparent PNG design and adjust placement on the mockup preview.

Tool: Printify product catalog.
Action: Select a domestic print provider located close to your target customers with a 9.5+ rating.
Finished Result: A high-quality digital product mockup showing the design on real garment colors.
6

Order a Physical Sample Order to Your Home

Before making a product public, order one sample for yourself at wholesale cost (usually $10 to $15). Check the print alignment, inspect color vibrancy, and run the garment through a normal wash and dry cycle. Experiencing your product firsthand prevents surprise customer complaints.

Tool: Printify "Order Sample" button.
Action: Pay wholesale sample cost and evaluate delivery speed and packaging quality.
Finished Result: Verified proof that the print quality meets high customer standards.
7

Calculate Realistic Retail Markup

Ensure every sale covers all expenses. If a blank t-shirt costs $11.00 and domestic shipping is $4.50, your base production expense is $15.50. Pricing the shirt at $25.00 leaves a $9.50 gross margin before marketplace transaction fees (roughly $2.50), leaving you an estimated $7.00 net profit.

Tool: Our interactive calculator below or a simple spreadsheet.
Action: Calculate total production + shipping + platform fees for every product.
Finished Result: A profitable retail price that prevents accidental loss on orders.
8

Connect Printify to Your Sales Channel

Navigate to Printify’s "Manage My Stores" tab and connect your sales channel (such as Etsy, Shopify, or TikTok Shop). Printify automatically syncs your product title, mockup photos, size variants, and inventory stock to your live store with one click.

Tool: Printify store integration tab.
Action: Authorize API connection between Printify and your marketplace storefront.
Finished Result: Automated listing sync ready to publish products to buyers.
9

Include Sizing Charts and Accurate Shipping Rules

Sizing confusion causes the vast majority of apparel complaints. Include an easy-to-read sizing chart image in inches and centimeters directly in your listing photos. Set handling times to 2–5 business days so buyers know custom printing takes a few days before dispatch.

Tool: Canva Free (Sizing Chart Template).
Action: Upload width and length garment dimensions as listing photo #2.
Finished Result: Clear buyer expectations that minimize size-related return requests.
10

Keep a Payment Buffer for Automated Fulfillment

When a customer orders on Etsy or Shopify, the customer’s money sits in your store balance until payout day (often 1 to 7 days). However, Printify charges your linked payment card immediately to start printing. Keep a small $50 to $100 buffer on your card to fulfill orders without delays.

Tool: Printify Billing settings tab.
Action: Link a payment card or balance and enable "Automatic Order Processing".
Finished Result: Orders print and dispatch automatically without manual intervention.

What Can Go Wrong (Honest Risks & Frustrations)

Print on demand protects you from unsold inventory, but operational issues do occur:

1. Trademark Infringement & Instant Store Bans:

Even innocent-sounding phrases can be actively trademarked by corporations. If a rights holder files an intellectual property takedown against your listing, marketplaces will issue strikes or ban your seller account permanently. Always search trademark databases first.

2. Holiday Production Bottlenecks:

During Q4 holiday rushes (Black Friday to Christmas), print facilities get overwhelmed. Orders that normally ship in 48 hours can take 6 to 9 days to print, leading to anxious customer messages. Set generous shipping estimates early in November.

3. Size Confusion & No-Return Policies:

Because items are custom printed, suppliers do not accept returns for customers who ordered the wrong size. You must either provide a replacement out of your own pocket or maintain strict, clear sizing charts on every product page.

4. Thin Margins After Heavy Ad Spend:

With roughly $7 to $10 in net margin per shirt, you cannot afford to spend $15 on paid ads to get one sale. Print on demand works best with organic search, social media showcase videos, or niche community outreach rather than paid advertising.

What to Expect in Your First 30, 60, and 90 Days

A realistic overview of what consistent daily effort looks like for a new print-on-demand creator:

Days 1 to 30

Niche Setup & 10 Listings

Create your first 10 original designs. Order 1 physical sample shirt to test print durability. Set up your Printify integration and publish listings. Expect 0 to 2 sales while search algorithms index your keywords.

Days 31 to 60

First Organic Sales

Expand your catalog to 25 products across t-shirts, sweatshirts, and mugs. You will see your first 3 to 10 organic sales ($20 to $70 net profit). Fulfill them smoothly and confirm tracking numbers scan on time.

Days 61 to 90

Catalog Optimization

Review which design styles and garment colors generate clicks. Discontinue designs that get zero interest and create variations on your top 2 sellers. Steady sales compound as your review count increases.

Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

  • Uploading low-resolution 72 DPI images: Phone screenshots or web thumbnails look blurry and jagged when printed on fabric. Always use 300 DPI graphics sized at 4500x5400px.
  • Listing copyrighted brand or pop-culture names: Never use names like Disney, Marvel, Taylor Swift, or sports franchises. You risk having your seller account permanently banned.
  • Choosing rough, cheap blank garments: Low-cost rough t-shirts cause negative reviews. Stick to reputable blanks like Bella+Canvas 3001 or Comfort Colors.
  • Forgetting to link a backup payment card: If your linked card runs out of funds, print orders will pause and lead to late shipping penalties on your store.
