How to Create & Sell an Online Course ($0 Startup)

Creating an online course means packaging a practical skill you know into step-by-step video lessons and worksheets that students pay to access. You can teach students live on video calls or record your lessons once so people can learn at their own pace.

Step-by-Step Execution Blueprint (10 Practical Steps)

Follow this 10-step plan to create, host, and sell your first digital learning mini-course.

1

Choose One Concrete, Outcome-Focused Skill

Do not build a vague, massive course on "Complete Graphic Design". Pick a specific skill with an immediate outcome: "How to Build a Monthly Family Budget in Google Sheets", "Beginner Fingerpicking Patterns on Guitar", or "Conversational French for Weekend Travelers".

Tool: A blank Google Doc.
Action: Define the exact transformation: "By the end of this course, you will be able to [specific task] in [specific timeframe]".
Finished Result: A clear, focused learning promise that prospective students can understand in one sentence.
2

Validate Demand First With Live Tutoring

Never spend 40 hours recording videos before knowing if people want to learn your topic. Offer a live 45-minute lesson for free or $15 to 2 friends, colleagues, or community members over Google Meet. Observe where they ask questions and what concepts confuse them.

Tool: Google Meet or Zoom Free.
Action: Teach 2 students live. Write down their questions word-for-word.
Finished Result: Tested teaching feedback that directly shapes your recorded curriculum.
3

Outline a 5-Module Mini-Curriculum in Google Docs

Break your topic into 5 sequential lessons, each 5 to 10 minutes long: Lesson 1: Overview and software setup. Lesson 2: Core fundamental rules. Lesson 3: Step-by-step hands-on build. Lesson 4: Troubleshooting common errors. Lesson 5: Finished project review and next steps.

Tool: Google Docs.
Action: Write 3 bullet points for each lesson outlining what you will say and demonstrate on screen.
Finished Result: A 5-lesson curriculum outline that keeps your recording focused and on track.
4

Design Clean Slide Decks in Canva Free

Use Canva to create clean 16:9 presentation slides. Keep slide text minimal—use large 32px+ headings, simple diagrams, and bullet points with no more than 4 lines per slide. Slide decks give visual learners an anchor while you speak.

Tool: Canva Free (Presentation 1920x1080).
Action: Create 3 to 5 slides per module. Export as PDF or present directly in your browser.
Finished Result: Clean visual slides ready to share on screen.
5

Record Screen and Voiceover in Free OBS Studio

Open OBS Studio. Add your screen capture and microphone input. Sit in a quiet room with soft furnishings to absorb echo. Record each lesson in a separate 5-to-10-minute video file. If you make a spoken mistake, pause for 3 seconds and repeat the sentence—you can easily snip it in editing.

Tool: OBS Studio (obsproject.com) or Loom Free (loom.com).
Action: Record lessons 1 through 5 in 1080p resolution.
Finished Result: 5 raw video recordings saved directly to your computer.
6

Trim and Polish Lessons in CapCut Free

Import your recorded clips into CapCut. Snip away false starts, coughing, and long dead pauses. Add subtle zoom-ins when demonstrating small buttons or formulas. Keep pacing snappy so students remain engaged. Export each finished lesson as an MP4 file.

Tool: CapCut Free.
Action: Label exported files cleanly: Lesson-01-Setup.mp4, Lesson-02-Basics.mp4, etc.
Finished Result: 5 crisp, professional video lessons ready for student access.
7

Create Practical Exercise Worksheets

Students learn by doing, not just watching. Create a 1-page summary PDF or template file (in Google Docs or Google Sheets) for each module. Include practice exercises and a checklist so students can test their new skills immediately after watching.

Tool: Google Docs or Canva Free.
Action: Export each worksheet as a clean PDF download.
Finished Result: Printable companion materials that add substantial perceived value to your course.
8

Upload Course Modules to Payhip Free Tier

Create a free account on Payhip (payhip.com). Click Add New Product > Course. Name your course, upload your 5 video lessons, attach the PDF worksheets to their respective modules, and write a clear course overview. Payhip handles student logins, video streaming, and automated progress tracking with zero monthly fee (they deduct a standard 5% per transaction).

Tool: Payhip Course Builder.
Action: Organize your 5 modules into a clean learning path and customize your sales page.
Finished Result: A fully functional online student portal with secure checkout and password-protected video access.
9

Set a Fair Introductory Price and Clear Refund Policy

Avoid pricing a beginner mini-course at $200. Set an accessible introductory price between $19 and $49 USD. Include a prominent 14-day money-back guarantee: "If you complete the exercises and do not find this course helpful, email me within 14 days for a full refund."

Tool: Payhip pricing tab & Stripe/PayPal connection.
Action: Set price to $29.00 USD and add your refund guarantee to the checkout page.
Finished Result: A low-friction checkout flow that reduces buyer hesitation.
10

Share Free Preview Lessons to Attract Students

Publish Lesson 1 for free on YouTube or your newsletter as a complete standalone tutorial. In the video description and pinned comment, write: "Enjoyed this setup? Get the full 5-part workshop and practice worksheets here: [Course Link]". This provides genuine upfront value while introducing your paid curriculum.

Tool: YouTube, social media, or free Substack.
Action: Upload your free preview lesson and link directly to your Payhip course checkout.
Finished Result: An organic student acquisition funnel based on proving your teaching ability first.

What Can Go Wrong (Honest Risks & Frustrations)

Teaching online is deeply rewarding, but you will encounter real operational challenges:

1. Low Initial Sales Without an Existing Audience:

Simply listing a course on Payhip or Gumroad will not bring students automatically. You must actively share free lessons, answer questions in community forums, or build an email list to direct students to your sales page.

2. Low Course Completion Rates:

Across the entire online education industry, only 10% to 20% of enrolled students finish every video. Keeping lessons bite-sized (under 10 minutes) and including practical worksheets significantly improves student completion.

3. Student Support and Q&A Burden:

Students will email you when they get stuck on an exercise or encounter software bugs. You must allocate 1 to 2 hours every week to reply patiently to student support messages.

4. Video Hosting File Limits:

Some free platforms limit the total gigabytes of video you can upload. Compressing your MP4 files in CapCut or HandBrake before uploading keeps file sizes small while maintaining crisp 1080p quality.

What to Expect in Your First 30, 60, and 90 Days

Building an online teaching business takes steady preparation. Here is a realistic timeline:

Days 1 to 30

Validation & Curriculum

Tutor 2 students live over Google Meet. Outline your 5 modules in Google Docs. Record your 5 lessons in OBS Studio and build your Payhip course page. Expect 0 to 2 sales during launch month.

Days 31 to 60

First Student Feedback

Share your free preview lesson on YouTube or social platforms. You might enroll 3 to 8 paying students ($90 to $240 total). Collect their honest feedback and add extra worksheets to clarify tricky steps.

Days 61 to 90

Organic Enrolments

Satisfied students leave positive testimonials. Your free preview videos begin ranking in search. You establish a steady flow of 5 to 15 course enrollments per month with minimal ongoing maintenance.

Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

  • Creating 30-hour comprehensive courses: Enormous courses overwhelm students and take months to record. Start with a tight 60-minute workshop solving one specific pain point.
  • Paying $99/month for course platforms before launch: Never pay monthly SaaS fees before you have validated student demand. Use free-tier platforms like Payhip or Gumroad.
  • Skipping live student testing: Recording in isolation leads to courses that answer questions nobody actually asked. Always teach someone live first.
  • Ignoring audio quality: Students will tolerate simple slides, but bad, muffled audio makes video lessons impossible to watch. Record in a quiet room with pillows nearby.
