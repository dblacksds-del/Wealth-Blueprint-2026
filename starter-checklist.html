Your Free 15-Point Beginner Launch Checklist

Welcome to your action checklist. Whether you are starting with $0 in freelancing or content, or testing digital templates, this 15-point launch tracker guides your first 30 days without expensive software or analysis paralysis.

LS
Written by Liam Senanayake. Reviewed October 2026.
Free companion checklist for readers launching their first digital venture.
Phase 1: Capital Assessment & Model Lock (Days 1–5)

Foundation & Direction

Review your current savings. If you have under $50, pick Freelancing, Affiliate Marketing, or UGC. If $500+, you can safely explore digital products or test physical models. Never risk essential living funds.
Pick one specific model and sign a personal commitment not to jump to new shiny objects or YouTube tutorials for 30 consecutive days.
Write down who pays you and what concrete headache you eliminate (e.g., "I edit 60-second vertical reels for busy podcasters so they gain reach without editing for hours").
Create a clean workspace using free accounts: Google Docs (drafting & client notes), Notion (task tracking), and Canva Free (visual assets). Do not purchase paid software subscriptions yet.
Phase 2: Work Samples & Minimum Portfolio (Days 6–12)

Proof of Competence

Clients buy proof, not resumes. Build 3 sample pieces (e.g., an edited video clip, rewritten landing page, or sample Notion template) showcasing real craftsmanship.
Package your 3 samples on a clean Notion page, Carrd free tier, or Google Doc published to the web. Ensure it loads cleanly and fast on mobile phones.
If selling physical or digital goods, search the USPTO Trademark Search (tmsearch.uspto.gov) or UK IPO register before using any phrase or slogan to avoid account bans.
Write a straightforward 3-line pitch message explaining the scope, turnaround time, flat pricing, and links to your 3 samples without using pushy sales buzzwords.
Phase 3: Outbound Outreach & Real Distribution (Days 13–22)

Taking Direct Action

Identify 25 local businesses, creators, or specific buyer keywords. Record contact names, website links, and one genuine compliment about their current work.
Commit to daily outbound work: 5 personalized emails, DMs, or 1 published educational asset every morning before consuming social media.
If a prospect hasn't replied after 4 business days, send a respectful 2-line check-in. If they do not respond, move on without pestering.
Deliver before your deadline. Communicate proactively. Provide clean, organized final files that make working with you effortless.
Phase 4: Feedback Loop & Unit Margins (Days 23–30)

Refinement & Growth

Ask your first client or customer: "What was the most helpful part of our work together?" Add their 2-sentence response directly to your portfolio.
Track real unit math. Subtract platform cuts, payment gateway fees (2.9% + $0.30), and sample costs from your gross revenue to understand your true take-home hourly rate.
Review your progress honestly. If you made money, double down on the single channel that produced it. If not, refine your work samples and double your outreach volume.
