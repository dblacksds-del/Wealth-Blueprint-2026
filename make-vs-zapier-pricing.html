Operations vs Tasks: Why the Math Catches Beginners Off Guard

Both Make.com and Zapier perform the exact same core function: when an event happens in Software A, they trigger a series of actions in Software B, C, and D. However, the two platforms calculate usage and monthly quota in fundamentally different ways. Understanding this difference prevents costly surprises on your client invoices.

How Make.com Counts "Operations"

In Make.com, every single bubble (called a "module") that executes on your visual canvas counts as one operation.

  • A webhook that receives data = 1 operation.
  • A search module that checks a database = 1 operation.
  • A router that sends data down 2 branches = 1 operation per executed path.
  • An email sending step = 1 operation.

How Zapier Counts "Tasks"

In Zapier, triggers are completely free. You are only billed when an action step successfully completes.

  • The trigger (e.g., new form submitted) = 0 tasks (free).
  • Action 1 (create Google Sheet row) = 1 task.
  • Action 2 (send SMS text) = 1 task.
  • Action 3 (send customer email) = 1 task.

At first glance, Zapier giving you free triggers sounds better. But look closely at the total quota included in each subscription: Make.com gives you 1,000 operations for $0 on its free tier and 10,000 operations for $9.00 USD per month on its Core plan. By contrast, Zapier gives you only 100 tasks on its free tier and charges $19.99 to $29.99 USD per month for only 750 tasks.

In real numbers: a standard 4-step lead automation running 200 times a month uses 800 operations in Make (fits 100% inside Make's free tier). In Zapier, that same workflow requires 600 tasks, blowing through Zapier's 100-task free limit by 600% and forcing the client onto a $240/year paid subscription.

Make.com Tier Breakdown: Free vs Core ($9/mo) vs Pro

Make.com (formerly known as Integromat) is currently the most cost-effective automation builder for small businesses and freelance service providers. Here is what each tier delivers:

Make Plan Monthly Cost Included Ops Execution Speed Best Use Case
Free Plan $0 Free 1,000 / month 15-minute polling Testing & low-volume local businesses
Core Plan $9.00 USD 10,000 / month 1-minute polling Standard client setups & active lead flows

Crucially, Make's Free Plan does not disable multi-step automation. You can create complex workflows with 10 different modules, data formatters, and conditional logic without paying a dime, provided your monthly usage stays under 1,000 operations.

Zapier Tier Breakdown: Free vs Starter vs Professional

Zapier is the grandfather of the automation industry. It integrates with more than 6,000 web applications and has the friendliest beginner interface on the market. However, its pricing structure penalizes multi-step systems heavily:

Zapier Plan Monthly Cost Included Tasks Multi-Step Zaps? Major Limitations
Free Plan $0 Free 100 / month No (2 steps only) Cannot do multi-step branching; 15-min update lag
Starter Plan $19.99 USD 750 / month Yes (Multi-step) No custom branching paths; 15-min update lag
Professional Plan $49.00 USD 2,000 / month Yes + Conditional Paths 2-minute polling; fast and reliable, but costly

The Real World Takeaway: If a client only needs one very simple connection (e.g., copy new Typeform answers directly into Mailchimp), Zapier's Free Plan can work. But the moment you want to format a phone number, check a CRM, or send two different emails based on client answers, Zapier requires at least their Starter or Professional plan ($240 to $588 per year).

Why You Must Always Build Inside the Client's Account

One of the biggest mistakes beginner freelancers make is creating a master Make or Zapier account and building every client's workflow inside their personal account. Here is why doing this creates massive financial and legal headaches:

  • You Absorb Surprise Volume Spikes: If a client runs a viral ad campaign and receives 15,000 lead submissions in one weekend, your personal credit card will be automatically charged overage fees by Make or Zapier.
  • Data Privacy & GDPR Violations: Holding customer names, phone numbers, and addresses from five different businesses inside one shared account mixes confidential records and creates legal liabilities.
  • Client Hostage Frustration: If a client wants to part ways or manage their business in-house, having their core business systems locked inside your personal account damages goodwill and creates awkward migrations.

The Professional Agency Protocol:

Always instruct the client to create their own free account on Make.com or Zapier and enter their corporate billing information. Then, have them send an invite to your business email as an Admin or Member. You do all development work inside their sandbox. If their usage grows, their company card handles the upgrade smoothly.

How to Scope and Price a $250 Lead Automation Setup

When pitching local companies (roofers, accountants, dental clinics, or real estate brokers), never sell "automation software" or "API connections". Business owners do not care about webhooks; they care about never losing a paying customer because an employee was slow to respond.

Package your service as a simple, high-value "Fast Lead Response System" for a fixed price of $250.00 USD. Here is the exact scope to deliver:

The Standard $250 Setup Deliverable Checklist:

  1. Step 1 (Form Capture): Connect their website contact form or Facebook Lead Ad form via webhook.
  2. Step 2 (Instant Lead Log): Automatically format the submission date, phone number, and customer notes into a clean Google Sheet CRM tab.
  3. Step 3 (Owner Alert): Send an immediate SMS text alert to the business owner's mobile phone containing the lead's name and phone number within 15 seconds of submission.
  4. Step 4 (Customer Confirmation): Send an automated branded confirmation email to the customer with the business's direct calendar booking link.
  5. Testing & Handover: Run 3 live test submissions, record a 2-minute Loom screen walkthrough showing the owner how to view their sheet, and hand over access.

This entire system takes an experienced builder about 45 minutes to construct, and takes a beginner about 2 to 3 hours following YouTube documentation. For a roofing company or legal practice where a single client is worth $2,000+, paying $250 one time to guarantee zero missed inquiries is an easy business decision.

Frequently Asked Questions About Make vs Zapier

Clear, realistic answers to essential automation software questions:

In Make.com, an operation is counted every single time a module performs an action, such as searching a row, updating a contact, or routing data. In Zapier, a task is only counted when a successful action step runs after the trigger. However, Zapier charges significantly more per task, while Make provides 10 times more operations for half the monthly cost.

Zapier's free plan is strictly limited to 100 tasks per month and only allows 2-step Zaps (one trigger and one action). It does not support multi-step branching, filters, or formatting tools. Real client workflows almost always require three or more steps, meaning clients will need a paid plan starting at $19.99 per month.

Always have the client pay for their own software subscriptions directly using their company credit card. You should be invited as a free admin or collaborator to their workspace. Never pay for client operations or software licenses on your personal card, as an unexpected volume spike could cause surprise charges that wipe out your earnings.

Make.com has a visual node-based canvas that looks slightly more complex at first glance. However, it gives you complete visual control over data mapping, routers, and error handlers. Most beginners can master basic lead workflows in Make within two to three days using free YouTube tutorials, saving their clients hundreds of dollars per year.

A standard beginner fixed price is $200 to $300 USD for a simple 3-step lead capture setup (for example: website contact form submission to Google Sheet log, instant SMS lead notification, and confirmation email). This takes two to three hours to build and test once you understand the tools.
